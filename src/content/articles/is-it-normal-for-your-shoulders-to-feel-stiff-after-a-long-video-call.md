<p>You end a long video call and both shoulders feel tight, elevated, or reluctant to drop. The stiffness is a frequent companion of prolonged seated screen work and usually reflects sustained low-level muscle activity rather than injury.</p>

<h2>What This Feels Like</h2>
<p>The tightness is most noticeable across the upper trapezius and the tops of the shoulders. Some people also sense a mild ache between the shoulder blades. It often improves within 10–20 minutes of standing, walking, and gently moving the arms. It is rarely sharp.</p>

<h2>Why This Happens</h2>
<p>During a video call the shoulders tend to stay slightly elevated or rounded while the arms rest on a desk or in the lap. The upper trapezius and levator scapulae remain active to support the head and arms in a relatively fixed posture. Work by Gwendolen Jull, Deborah Falla, and others on cervical and scapular muscle behavior has shown how prolonged static postures increase fatigue and perceived stiffness in these muscles even when the absolute load is light.</p>

<h2>When to Worry</h2>
<p>Consult a clinician if stiffness is one-sided and progressive, accompanied by arm numbness or weakness, follows a distinct injury, or does not ease with ordinary movement over several days.</p>

<h2>How to Manage</h2>
<p>Simple interruptions help:</p>
<ul>
<li>Stand and roll the shoulders backward and downward every 20–30 minutes.</li>
<li>Let the arms hang briefly at your sides between calls.</li>
<li>Adjust the screen so you are not craning the neck forward.</li>
<li>A short walk after the call often resets the resting shoulder position.</li>
</ul>

<h2>FAQs</h2>
<h3>Why do both shoulders feel equally stiff?</h3>
<p>The posture is usually symmetrical, so both sides experience similar sustained activity.</p>
<h3>Does a standing desk prevent it?</h3>
<p>It can reduce the problem if you still change position regularly; standing still for long periods can create its own stiffness.</p>
<h3>Is this related to stress?</h3>
<p>Mildly. Concentration and mild tension can raise baseline shoulder muscle activity, amplifying the effect of the static posture.</p>

<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>Shoulder stiffness after a long video call is a normal response to sustained static posture. Brief movement breaks and a short walk afterward usually restore comfortable resting tone.</p>
